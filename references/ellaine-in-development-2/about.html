We built Fathom because research was broken

The timing problem

Reports that take months to land, arrive after the call’s already made, and get filed away more often than opened. Most of us have sat in that meeting.

The direction problem

Other times it isn’t timing — it’s not knowing which customers to prioritize or where the real opportunity is hiding. That doubt rarely raises a red flag. It just stalls the room, or lets whoever’s loudest decide.

Why Fathom exists

Closing that gap — timing or direction — is the job. We hand back more than findings: the accuracy to trust what you’re seeing, the confidence to act on it, and sometimes an angle nobody in the room had considered.

Why research matters

A decision gets made either way. Be the one that makes it on the data that matters.

Most companies aren’t short on data anymore — dashboards, surveys, transaction histories. What’s harder to find is which of it actually matters.

That’s where we spend our time — not handing back everything we found, but pointing to:

  • A direction worth testing
  • A segment worth prioritizing
  • An assumption worth revisiting
What we believe

3 things we don’t compromise on.

Judgment.

Curiosity.

Honesty.

Human judgment isn’t automatable.

We answer the question underneath the question.

We say what we see.

Tools can move fast through information. They can’t recognize what matters, know your company’s politics, or make the call you’d stand behind in a room. That’s still a human job, and we take it seriously.

Clients rarely hand us the real question first — they hand us the version they know how to phrase. Part of the job is finding the one underneath it, and staying curious past the first answer that technically fits.

Even when it challenges the brief, the hypothesis, or the answer everyone in the room was hoping for. A finding you can’t trust isn’t an insight — it’s decoration.
