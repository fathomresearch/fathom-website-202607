The setup

Every number in the deck was healthy. Every one of them was falling.

{{SETUP_LEAD}} — market leadership, the best-known brand in the category, and a board pack that opened with all three facts. None of them was wrong.

Boards read levels, and levels are the last thing to move. A brand can lose the argument for four years before it loses a point of share, because the people who already chose it keep choosing it out of habit while the people forming a habit for the first time go elsewhere. By the time the level falls, the cause is half a decade old.

So the question we took to seven years of tracking was not who is ahead. It was who is moving, how fast, and what happens if nobody does anything about it.

How the tracker was kept honest

The question has not changed since 2019. That is the method.

Held constant, by contract
  • Wording, to the comma.
  • The scale, its labels and its length.
  • The order of the brand list, rotated the same way each time.
  • Mode — online, same panel provider, same device mix.
  • Fieldwork weeks, so seasonality lands identically.
  • Quotas and weighting targets, re-based only when the census is.

Almost every broken tracker was broken by an improvement. Someone tightens the wording, adds a brand, moves to a shorter scale, switches panel — each defensible on its own, and each one puts a step in the series that looks exactly like a change in the market. We would rather run a question we would write differently today than lose the ability to compare it to 2019.

When comparability does break, we flag it and leave it broken. A wave that cannot be compared is reported as its own reading with the reason attached, never quietly spliced onto the line before it. A trend with a hidden join in it is worse than no trend, because it will be believed.

On the share chart the two brands nearest you are C and A. On this one they are not on the axis at all — both are falling faster than you are, so the gap widens every year they exist. The two that arrive are the two smallest companies in the category, and the first of them is currently a third of your size. These years are not forecasts. Each is arithmetic: the gap in share divided by the gap in annual rate, across four comparable waves, and we show it that way on purpose. The point is not 2030. It is that the order on this axis is the reverse of the order on every chart the board currently reads.
