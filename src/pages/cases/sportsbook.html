Case 02 Gaming & Sports Betting Synthetic panel

The brief changed. The pitch was a week out.

Months of segmentation were already done. The question was whether any of it could answer something new in time.

Client
National sportsbook operator
Industry
Gaming & sports betting
Engagement
Synthetic panel from existing segmentation
Method
On-demand strategy tool
The situation

The research was finished. The question it had been built to answer was not the question anymore.

A major sportsbook had spent months segmenting U.S. sports bettors. Three distinct audiences, with real depth on values, needs and motivations. Then the creative brief changed.

Fielding a fresh study meant six weeks if everything went well. The pitch was in one. The strategy lead already suspected Segment 2 would reject the new angle, but suspecting it is not the same as being able to walk into the room and defend it.

The timeline

What we did with the week.

Brief

The creative direction moves.

The segmentation had answered the old brief well. Against the new one it could only be guessed at, and guessing was what the team was trying to avoid.

Call

We stop proposing a new study.

Six weeks of fresh fieldwork was the obvious answer and the wrong one. The data to answer this already existed. It just could not be asked anything.

Build

The segmentation becomes a panel.

We rebuilt their existing responses into a synthetic panel: the three segments, grounded in what real customers had actually told them, in a form the team could question directly.

Test

The new angle goes in front of Segment 2.

The hunch held up, and now it came with evidence attached. The angle was reworked before it reached artwork rather than after.

Pitch

The team walks in with the data behind them.

Not a hunch about how Segment 2 would react, but a defensible read on it, sourced from research the client had already paid for.

How it was possible

We stopped treating the finished study as a finished thing. The fastest research is often the research you have already done.

Delivered

A panel the strategy team could interrogate on their own schedule. New messaging tested the same night it was written.

No lengthy brief required. If you know the decision and the date, that’s enough to start.
