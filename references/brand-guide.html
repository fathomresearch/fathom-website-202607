Part I · The approach

Twelve lenses, in the order they should be asked.

A brand is not evaluated as one thing. It is read through a stack of separate questions, and the order matters: a beautiful palette on a brand that cannot say what it sells is a decorated void. So the lenses run outside-in — what the brand means, then how it is made, then whether it holds up under repetition, deadline and scrutiny. Everything in Part II is an answer to one of these.

Part II · Positioning

The promise is a refusal before it is a claim.

Fathom does not sell research. It sells the end of a specific frustration: the study that arrives after the decision, and the deck nobody opens twice. Every page states the claim by first naming what it refuses — and the refusal is always concrete enough to be checked.

The enemy, named

Six-week timelines. Beautiful decks with no recommendation. Stated preference mistaken for truth. Research filed in a shared drive. The room where the loudest voice decides.

The claim

Depth and speed are not a trade-off. AI does the processing; researchers do the judgment. What comes back is a recommendation you can defend, not a readout.

The proof, always countable

48-hour category scan. Two-to-three-week strategic study. Four-to-ten-week engagements. A handful of pro bono organisations a year, chosen by us.

The rule this creates: never write a Fathom claim that cannot be falsified. “World-class insight” is not a position; “a category scan in 48 hours” is. If a new line cannot be checked by a client with a calendar, it is decoration — cut it or make it countable.

Verbal identity

Two clauses. The second one turns.

Fathom’s headline is not a sentence, it is a manoeuvre. A plain statement sets an expectation; an em dash or a full stop pivots; the second clause reverses it. The turn is always the italic, always the lighter tone. Once you see it, it is on every page.

FORM   [ plain statement, roman ]  —  [ the turn, italic, one tone lighter ]

Research should tell you what to do — not just what happened.Home · hero

Data tells you what happened, our insights tell you what to do next.What We Do · hero

Depth and speed aren’t a trade-off. They’re the point.Home · proposition

The fans were loyal. Nobody knew what they were loyal to.Case · motorsports

The brief changed on a Friday. The pitch was Tuesday.Case · sportsbook

They had a theory. Nobody had tested it.Case · wealth

Always

Sentence case headlines. Active voice, present tense, second person. The em dash as the pivot — it is the brand’s punctuation mark. Concrete nouns. One idea per section. Title Case only on buttons, nav links and eyebrows.

Never

Exclamation marks. Hype adjectives — leverage, unlock, best-in-class, game-changing. Title Case headlines. All-caps beyond a micro-label. Hedged findings. A second metaphor in the same page.

Reserved lines

Clarity Beneath the Surface is the footer close — italic Spectral, never promoted to a headline. fathom (n./v.) — the dictionary device that licenses the depth metaphor. Use it once, on the page that needs to explain the name.

Do — refuse gracefully

A handful of organisations a year, chosen by us.

Limits are stated flatly and made part of the offer. Scarcity without apology reads as standards, not sales.

Don’t — inflate

We partner with mission-driven organisations to unlock transformative impact.

Nothing here can be checked, nothing is refused, and no reader is told what they get. This is the register the whole brand is built against.

Mark & lockup

A wordmark, a sounding, and a descriptor that whispers.

Research & Strategy

Primary lockup on navy.
Wordmark 28–30px in nav, 22px in footer. Descriptor centred beneath, never beside.

The symbol: a sphere cut by a waterline, with the depth mark rising through it. Standalone use only where the name is already established — favicon, avatar, stamp.

Rules

Three colourways only: teal on navy, navy on light, white on photography. Clear space equals the cap height of the wordmark on every side. The descriptor Research & Strategy is set in tracked micro-caps at roughly a quarter of the wordmark height, centred, at 68% opacity on navy and 28% in the footer — it is a whisper, not a second line of the logo.

Never: recolour outside the three ways, set the descriptor beside the wordmark, place the mark on a mid-tone photograph without a scrim, or rebuild it in a system font.

Colour

One accent, spent almost nowhere.

Fathom is a two-value brand — a deep navy ground and a single teal — carried by a warm neutral family that keeps it from reading as tech. The discipline is not in the hexes; it is in the budget. Teal covers roughly one to three percent of any screen. Everything else is ground, ink and hairline.

The one rule that matters

Depth and speed aren’t a trade-off. They’re the point.

Depth and speed aren’t a trade-off. They’re the point.

#00D6B3 on navy, #009E85 on paper. The accent changes value when the ground changes so it stays the same colour perceptually — and so it stays readable.

What breaks it

Bright teal set as text on a light ground.

Challenge Us See The Work Get In Touch

1.86:1 — unreadable, and it makes the brand look like a start-up. Three equal CTAs in one view spends the accent budget and leaves the reader with no decision to make. One primary action per screen.

Where teal is allowed

The eyebrow dash. The 2px rule that opens a data block. One CTA fill. The italic turn in a dark headline. The single highlighted metric. One active state.

Where it is not

Body copy. Backgrounds larger than a button. Icons for their own sake. More than one emphasis in a single headline. Anything decorative.

Semantic colour

Fathom has no red/amber/green system and does not need one. In charts, the diverging pair is teal against struck grey, with --line as the neutral midpoint — and colour is always paired with a glyph and a word.

Typography

Three families. Three jobs. No overlap.

Spectral carries the argument, IBM Plex Sans carries the information, IBM Plex Mono carries the instrumentation. The pairing works because Plex Sans and Plex Mono are siblings — the mono voice is a register of the body voice, not a fourth identity imported for texture.

Spectral Display only. Headlines, sub-heads, pull quotes, dossier values, the footer tagline. Never body copy. 200 / 300 / 400 · italic 200 / 300

Research should tell you what to do — not just what happened.

Tracking tightens as size grows: -.02em at 30px, -.035em at 88px. Line-height 1.02–1.18. Weight drops as size grows — 200 is for the largest lines on dark grounds, 400 for card titles and dossier values.

IBM Plex Sans Body, UI, buttons, nav, form fields, and the oversized metric numerals. 400 / 500 / 600 / 700

Most research firms make you choose. Go deep and wait months. Move fast and accept shallow. We built Fathom because that choice is false. AI handles the processing. Our researchers handle the judgment.

15–18px, line-height 1.68–1.78, measure capped at 640–660px. 500 for buttons and active nav, 600 for micro-labels, 700 reserved for the big metric figures — the only place a heavy weight appears.

IBM Plex Mono Instrumentation. Eyebrows, data labels, units, tags, filing keys, the noise field. 400 / 500 · always tracked

Where we fit  /  fm = fathoms of engagement  /  ▼ 3 fm  /  Exhibit B  /  Method

10–12px, letter-spacing .14–.22em, uppercase. Mono signals measured — it labels things that were counted, filed or instrumented. It never sets a sentence.

Loading: the three families are pulled from Google Fonts with preconnect to both hosts and display=swap, and every page requests only the weights it actually uses. Spectral’s fallback stack is Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif — a genuine transitional serif, so a failed load degrades in proportion rather than dropping to a default sans.

Layout & ground rhythm

A long page is paced by its grounds.

Fathom pages are not built from cards on a single background. They are built as a sequence of full-bleed bands whose tone changes, and the rhythm does the pacing work that a sidebar or an illustration would do elsewhere. One rule governs it: no two dark bands touch.

Frame

Fixed 72px navy nav with a matching spacer element. Content capped at 1200px on marketing pages, 1240px on case pages. Gutter clamp(24px, 6vw, 80px).

Vertical scale

Standard band 96px, large 128px, tight 44–56px. Newer pages express the same intervals as clamp(80px, 13vh, 150px) so tall viewports get more air.

Radii

0 for full-bleed blocks and bars. 2px tags. 3px photo frames. 4px buttons and inputs. 8px cards. 100px pills only. Nothing is fully rounded.

Hairlines

.5px on dark grounds, 1px on light. Grids are built from 1px gaps in a --line-coloured container rather than per-cell borders — corners never double up.

Signature devices

The kit that makes a page recognisable before it is read.

These are the brand’s most portable assets — more so than the logo, which appears twice a page at 28px. Each one is rendered live below in its real CSS. Use two or three per page; using all of them at once is how a page starts looking like a demo of itself.

Imagery & data

Photography never carries the colour.

Every photograph on the site is desaturated before it is allowed on the page, then tinted back toward the brand. This is what keeps licensed stock from fighting the palette — and it means an image can be swapped without a colour review. The filter stack is fixed; only the picture changes.

Client logos

Greyscale at 72% opacity, sitting in an even hairline rail. They return to full colour on hover only. A logo wall is a claim about company, not a design element — it stays quiet until touched.

People

Team portraits are the one exception: full colour, 4:5, 3px radius, in a fixed frame so uneven source crops still line up. Radical humanity is a stated value; the faces are not filtered.

Charts

Static editorial visuals, hand-built, no chart library. Length or position carries the value and the printed figure is only a label. Diverging scales run teal to struck-grey. Never a dual axis, never colour without a glyph and a word.

One picture per case

A case page earns exactly one signature visualisation, chosen from the research method’s own native artifact. The geometry appears once — the cover does not preview it and the outcome does not redraw it.

Motion

One easing curve, used almost everywhere.

Motion in this system is not expressive; it is a reading aid. Content arrives slightly late and slightly low, so the eye is led down the page in the order the argument is written. Everything shares cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1) — a fast start with a long settle.

The reveal

opacity 0→1 with translateY(24px→0) over 700–800ms, staggered in 70–100ms steps. Driven by one IntersectionObserver at a 0.1–0.18 threshold that unobserves after firing — elements never re-animate on scroll-back.

The resolve

Reserved for the home hero. Blur 16px to 0 with letter-spacing easing from .12em to -.02em. Literal: noise resolving into signal. Lines are set white-space: nowrap so the tracking swing can never re-wrap them mid-animation.

The strike

On a case-page turn, a rule draws across the expected answer — width 0 to 100% over 700ms — then the reframed question resolves out of blur. Orchestrated in sequence, not fired at once.

Non-negotiable

Every animated rule has a prefers-reduced-motion counterpart that lands content in its final state. Hover is never the only route to information — the What We Do instrument panel opens a tap popup below 900px for exactly this reason.

Part III · Contrast audit

Computed, not eyeballed.

Every real foreground/background pairing on the live site, run through the WCAG 2.1 ratio. Normal text needs 4.5:1; large text — 24px, or 18.66px bold — needs 3:1. Most of the system is comfortably clear. Four pairings are not, and they are all low-opacity white on navy or teal-dim used below its size floor.

If you read nothing else

Nine rules that keep a new page on-brand.

OneWrite the turn firstA Fathom headline is two clauses where the second reverses the first. If your line has no turn, it is not finished.
TwoMake the claim countable48 hours. Two to three weeks. A handful a year. If a client with a calendar cannot check it, cut it.
ThreeSpend teal onceOne eyebrow, one rule, one button, one italic. Bright teal on dark, dim teal on light, never the reverse.
FourThree families, three jobsSpectral argues, Plex Sans informs, Plex Mono measures. A mono sentence or a serif paragraph is a bug.
FivePace with groundsNavy, paper, warm, navy. No two dark bands touch, and navy-deep appears at most once.
SixPick two devicesFrom the kit of eight. Using six on one page reads as a style demo, not a piece of work.
SevenDesaturate the photoGreyscale, then tint back. No image is ever allowed to introduce a colour the palette does not have.
EightOne easing curvecubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1), 24px up, 700–800ms, staggered. And it all switches off on request.
NineCompute the contrastBefore shipping, not after. The brand’s own promise is that it says what it sees — the page should be readable enough to prove it.
