The setup

The brand had a story. The market had a better one, and it was already public.

{{SETUP_LEAD}} — the brief wanted to know how a set of messages was landing. The messages were the brand’s: performance, ecosystem, value, the three things every deck in the category leads with.

A survey can only return answers to questions you already thought to ask. Ask people to rate your three messages and they will rate your three messages, and you will never learn that the conversation they are actually having is about a fourth thing you have never mentioned. To find that, you cannot ask. You have to listen to what is said when no researcher is in the room.

So we took eighteen months of unprompted, public conversation — not a panel, not a prompt, just people talking to each other — and read what they brought up on their own.

How the conversation was read

A machine counts words. It does not know when you are joking.

“great. another update that breaks the one thing I used.”

Positive→Coded negative“great” is sarcasm; the sentence is a complaint

“not the worst thing I’ve paid for this year, honestly.”

Negative→Coded mildly positivea double negative and a hedge, read as faint praise

“ok this is genuinely sick, I’d die for the little guy.”

Negative→Coded strongly positive“sick” and “die” are affection in this register

An automated sentiment score is a starting point, not a finding. Off-the-shelf tools miss sarcasm, negation and in-group slang — the three things people use most when they actually care. We hand-code a representative sample, train the model on that, and then a human audits the confident-but-wrong tail, because that is precisely where the interesting lines hide.

And we bound the universe before we count it. Bots and repost chains are removed, and so is the brand’s own output and its PR pickup — otherwise you spend the study listening to yourself and mistaking it for the market. What is left is people, talking unprompted, to each other.

Performance, ecosystem, value — the brand’s own three messages — appear nowhere on this wall. The loudest half of the conversation is emotional and experiential: what it is like to live with the thing, not what it scores on a spec sheet. The complaints are specific enough to fix and the affection is specific enough to build on, and neither would have surfaced from a survey that asked about the three things the brand already believed.
