Sports Betting & iGaming Segmentation & Behavioral Research

You don’t have a value tier. You have three players.

For years, the operator’s segmentation ran on one number: how much a player wagered. It could tell the business who was worth courting — but it couldn’t tell creative who these people actually were.

The trap

A value tier tells you what someone’s worth. It doesn’t tell you who they are.

The operator’s segmentation ran on one axis: how much a player wagered each month. It told the business exactly who to court financially — but it gave creative nothing to say. Every player above a threshold got the same premium message, whether they wanted status, a deal, or just a good time.

So we stopped sorting by dollars and started sorting by disposition — and three distinct types of player fell out of the data, each chasing a different feeling from the same wager.

How we built it

We didn’t rank them by spend. We typed them by disposition.

01 · Design
Divide, don’t deposit-rank

We profile on the attitudes and behaviors that predict how someone plays — not the deposit size that only describes what they’ve already spent.

02 · Field
Two waves, one typing tool

An attitudinal & behavioral study defined the types; a second, larger fielding of 2,142 active bettors validated them — sourced through the operator’s own player base and an external consumer panel, across every legal U.S. market plus Canada.

03 · Analyze
Let the types emerge

A behavioral clustering model, not predefined value bands — the types fell out of how people actually play, not where we expected the cutoffs to be.

04 · Validate
Built to brief, not just to describe

Every type was pressure-tested against real behavior — handle, tenure, channel, sport, and promo response — so creative and CRM could build against it, not just read about it.

Back in the field

Naming three types was the start. We went back to find out what moves each one.

Once the three types existed, we went back into the field with a second, purpose-built study aimed at each one specifically — testing how reward types and message framing land, type by type, instead of testing the market as a whole. The output: a type-by-type map of what actually moves each player to act, not just who they are.
